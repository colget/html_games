How to remember basic strategy

The big idea: assume the dealer's hidden card is a 10.
  • Dealer shows 2 to 6 → a weak dealer. They often bust, so play safe and let them.
  • Dealer shows 7 to Ace → a strong dealer. You need a real hand to beat them.
"Small card, they might fall. Big card, you must go for it."

1. Hard hands (no Ace, or Ace counts as 1)

  • 17 or more → always STAND
  • 13 – 16 → STAND v 2–6, HIT v 7–A Weak dealer: sit tight. Strong dealer: take a card.
  • 12 → STAND only v 4, 5, 6, otherwise HIT 12 is the fussy one: it only stands against the middle three.
  • 11 → DOUBLE (v Ace: hit if the dealer hits soft 17)
  • 10 → DOUBLE v 2–9, HIT v 10 or A Double 10 unless the dealer shows a 10 or Ace.
  • 9 → DOUBLE v 3–6, otherwise HIT
  • 8 or less → always HIT

2. Soft hands (an Ace counting as 11)

  • Soft 19 – 20 (A,8 / A,9) → always STAND
  • Soft 18 (A,7) → DOUBLE v 3–6, STAND v 2, 7, 8, HIT v 9, 10, A Soft 18 is the tricky one: learn it on its own.
  • Soft 17 (A,6) → DOUBLE v 3–6, otherwise hit. Never stand on soft 17.
  • Soft 15 – 16 (A,4 / A,5) → DOUBLE v 4–6, otherwise hit
  • Soft 13 – 14 (A,2 / A,3) → DOUBLE v 5–6, otherwise hit
The doubling window gets smaller as the hand gets smaller: A,7 and A,6 double v 3–6 → A,5 and A,4 v 4–6 → A,3 and A,2 v 5–6. Dealer 5 and 6 are the weakest cards, so they're always in.

3. Pairs

  • Always split A,A and 8,8. Never split 10s or 5s (play 5,5 as a hard 10). "Aces and eights, split them straight. Fives and tens, never again."
  • 9,9 → SPLIT v 2–9 except 7. Stand v 7, 10, A. Why not v 7? Your 18 already beats the dealer's likely 17.
  • 7,7 → SPLIT v 2–7  ·  6,6 → SPLIT v 2–6 The pair's number is the highest dealer card you split against.
  • 2,2 and 3,3 → SPLIT v 2–7, otherwise hit
  • 4,4 → just HIT

4. Can't double? (3 or more cards)

Just HIT instead, except soft 18: STAND instead.

Practice tips

  • Learn it in this order: hard hands → pairs → soft hands.
  • Test yourself: in Settings, untick "Show strategy recommendation". Choose your move, then check the chart.
  • The Full chart tab highlights your current hand in pink.

Hi-Lo card counting

Counting tells you whether the cards left in the shoe favour you or the dealer. It is separate from basic strategy: you still play your hand the same way.

1. Give every card a value

  • 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 → +1 (small cards gone = good for you)
  • 7, 8, 9 → 0 (ignore them)
  • 10, J, Q, K, A → −1 (big cards gone = bad for you)
"Low cards up, high cards down."

2. Keep a running count

Add up the values of every card you see. Start at 0 when the shoe is shuffled. A full deck adds up to exactly 0.

3. Turn it into a true count

True count = running count ÷ decks left in the shoe.
Example: running count +6 with 3 decks left = true count +2.

4. What it means

  • True count +1 or less: the house has the edge. Bet small.
  • True count +2 and up: lots of 10s and Aces left, which helps you. Players who count usually bet more here.

Practice tip

Untick "Show running and true count" in Settings, keep the count in your head, then switch it back on to check yourself.
