How the pieces move

  • ♚︎ King: one square in any direction. Never onto a square that is attacked.
  • ♛︎ Queen: any number of squares in a straight line or diagonal.
  • ♜︎ Rook: any number of squares up, down, left or right.
  • ♝︎ Bishop: any number of squares diagonally. It stays on one colour all game.
  • ♞︎ Knight: an "L" shape, 2 squares one way then 1 to the side. The only piece that can jump over others.
  • ♟︎ Pawn: one square forward (two on its first move). Captures one square diagonally forward.

Special moves

  • Castling: the king moves two squares towards a rook, and the rook jumps to the other side of it. Only allowed if neither has moved, nothing is between them, and the king is not in check or passing through an attacked square. Click the king, then the square two to the side.
  • Promotion: a pawn that reaches the far end becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight (usually a queen).
  • En passant: if a pawn moves two squares and lands right beside your pawn, you can capture it as if it had moved only one, but only on the very next move.

Winning and drawing

  • Check: the king is under attack. You must get out of check straight away.
  • Checkmate: in check with no way out. The game is over.
  • Stalemate: not in check, but no legal moves. It's a draw.
  • Also drawn: the same position three times, 50 moves each with no capture or pawn move, or not enough pieces left to checkmate.

Piece values

Pawn 1 · Knight 3 · Bishop 3 · Rook 5 · Queen 9. Don't swap a bigger piece for a smaller one. The number beside the captured pieces shows who is ahead.

Opening tips

  • Take the centre. Start with a centre pawn (e4 or d4).
  • Get your knights and bishops out before moving the same piece twice.
  • Castle early to keep your king safe.
  • Don't bring your queen out too soon. It just gets chased around.

Before every move, ask yourself

  • What is my opponent threatening with their last move?
  • Are any of my pieces undefended or attacked?
  • Can I give check, capture something, or make a threat?

Using this page to learn

  • Press 💡 Hint to see the computer's suggestion (highlighted in blue).
  • Press 👁 Covered to see which squares each side controls: a white dot means a White piece covers that square, a black dot means a Black piece does. A piece with only the other colour's dot on it is under attack and undefended.

    A good way to use it:

    1. Before each move, look for any of your pieces with only the opponent's dot on it, and protect or move it.
    2. Then look at the opponent's pieces the same way. A piece with only your dot on it can be captured for free.
  • Made a mistake? Press Undo and try something else.
  • Start on Easy. Move up a level when you win most games.
